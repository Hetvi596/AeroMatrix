# Sensor Intelligence (Task 2): missing sensors and unseen locations

Checked against the app source on 11 October 2026 (backend/task2_model/aeromatrix_service.py and src/task2/SensorsPanel.tsx).

## What Sensor Intelligence does and which stations it supports
Sensor Intelligence is opened from "Sensors" in the app's feature launcher. It estimates the next hour of the two particulate pollutants at a monitoring station whose sensor is missing or offline, using readings from nearby stations. The user picks a station, an hour and a scenario, presses Run prediction, and sees three cards: the two particulate forecasts and an AQI card. Supported stations: the five Pune stations with verified coordinates, Revenue Colony-Shivajinagar, Hadapsar, Katraj Dairy, Panchawati Pashan and Dhankawadi. The other eight stations, including the Pimpri-Chinchwad ones, have no verified coordinates and are not supported. All results are historical replays of the February to September 2025 test period, not live data.

## Sensor Intelligence models and reliability
Twelve trained scikit-learn models from the Task 2 package run in a local Python service on port 8030: for each particulate pollutant, one network model and one whole-station holdout model per verified station. Forecasts are one hour ahead in micrograms per cubic metre, with an interval calibrated for about 90% coverage across the evaluation, not a guaranteed probability for one hour. The model needs at least two nearby stations with a reading in the last two hours and one within 20 km; otherwise it abstains and shows "No forecast" rather than guessing. The value recorded later for the forecast hour is shown for comparison but is never a model input. In normal hours, repeating the station's last reading (persistence) was more accurate than the model, so the model is meant for hours when that reading is missing. Accuracy for unseen stations varies widely, and citywide or live accuracy is not established.

## Sensor Intelligence scenarios and failure simulation
"Missing sensor" uses the network model. "Unseen location" uses the model trained without that station and withholds the station's own readings. In the failure simulation the user can switch nearby sensors off, mark the station's own sensor offline, or apply the package's evaluated reading-loss scenarios: 0, 10, 30, 50 or 70% of readings removed, in random, 12-hour block or whole-station patterns, with seeds 17, 29 or 43. Removing too many sensors makes the model abstain.

## How the Sensor Intelligence AQI is calculated
The AQI is calculated with the CPCB breakpoint formula on 24-hour averages, so it is an estimated PM-based AQI, not an official CPCB AQI. For each particulate pollutant the app averages the 24 hours ending at the forecast hour: the 23 recorded hours before it plus the one-hour forecast, with at least 16 valid hours required. The sub-index is linear interpolation within the CPCB band, and the AQI is the higher of the two sub-indices. Categories: Good 0-50, Satisfactory 51-100, Moderately Polluted 101-200, Poor 201-300, Very Poor 301-400, Severe 401-500. Because the forecast is only one of 24 hours, the recorded readings drive most of the AQI. An official CPCB AQI needs at least three pollutants, and this data has only the two particulate ones, so the card says "not official". When data are short, in the unseen-location scenario, or when the model abstains, it shows "AQI unavailable — insufficient pollutant data".

## Budget sensitivity for missing-sensor forecasts
A hypothetical block runs the package's planner on the fine-particle forecast: which priced actions fit a budget, and whether that choice changes at the interval's ends, with effects 30% weaker or stronger, with a 30% smaller budget or without the top action. Costs come from Plan-It prices and effects from the rule-based scenario engine; the Task 2 model learned no intervention effects.
